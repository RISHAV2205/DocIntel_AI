The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) plays a key role in regulating and developing the capital market. Its major functions are:
Regulatory Function: Regulates the activities of stock exchanges, brokers, and other intermediaries.
Protective Function: Protects investors from unfair trade practices and frauds.
Developmental Function: Promotes training, research, and fair trading practices to develop the market.

2. Causes and Consequences of the Revolt of 1857
Causes:
Political Causes:
The British policy of annexation (e.g., Doctrine of Lapse) and dethroning Indian rulers created deep resentment.
Economic Causes:
Heavy taxation, destruction of traditional industries, and exploitation of peasants and artisans led to poverty.
Military Causes:
Indian soldiers (sepoys) faced discrimination in pay, promotion, and conditions. The use of greased cartridges offended religious sentiments.
Social and Religious Causes:
Fear of forced conversion to Christianity and interference with traditional customs.
Immediate Cause:
The introduction of Enfield rifle cartridges greased with cow and pig fat triggered open rebellion.
Consequences:
End of Company Rule:
The British East India Company was abolished, and India came under the direct rule of the British Crown.
Administrative Changes:
The post of Secretary of State for India was created in the British government.
Army Reorganization:
The British increased European troops and reduced Indian soldiers to prevent future revolts.
Policy of Divide and Rule:
The British encouraged division among Hindus and Muslims to weaken unity.
National Awakening:
The revolt marked the beginning of Indian nationalism and inspired later freedom movements.
