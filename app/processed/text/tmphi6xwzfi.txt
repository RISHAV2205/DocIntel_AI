ABSTRACT
This paper presents an AI-driven smart shopping cart system designed to enhance retail
efficiency and customer experience through real-time data analytics and machine learning. Traditional
shopping carts lack capabilities for adaptive tracking, inventory management, and personalized customer
interaction. Our system addresses these gaps with a multi-layered architecture that integrates person-
specific tracking, reinforcement learning (RL) for navigation, and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)
networks for demand forecasting, alongside seamless Point-of-Sale (POS) integration for automated billing.
The architecture comprises real-time data capture, edge computing for low-latency decisions, and cloud
processing for customer profiling and inventory management. Experimental results demonstrate notable
improvements in tracking accuracy, navigation efficiency, inventory forecasting, and customer satisfaction,
highlighting AI’s transformative potential in retail.

within this ecosystem is the AI-powered smart shopping cart,
a system poised to streamline the entire in-store shopping
process—from product selection to automated billing—

II. BACKGROUND AND RELATED WORK
The swift progress in AI and ML has significantly impacted
the retail industry, introducing solutions that enhance
operational efficiency, improve customer satisfaction, and
streamline inventory management [9], [10]. AI is widely
applied in decision-making systems, while ML models excel
in predictive tasks, learning from both historical and real-
time data. The integration of these technologies has enabled
the development of sophisticated systems that automate
various retail processes, such as customer interaction, product
recommendation, and navigation [11]. Smart shopping carts
equipped with AI and ML have, in particular, attracted
attention for their potential to transform the traditional
shopping experience.
A. SMART SHOPPING CARTS
Smart shopping carts are engineered to automate and improve
different aspects of in-store shopping, featuring capabilities
such as product scanning, navigation, and billing automation.
Early implementations, like Amazon Dash Cart [12] and
Caper’s Smart Cart [13], focused on checkout simplification
by eliminating manual product scanning through RFID tech-
nology, barcode scanners, and computer vision algorithms.
However, these systems lacked advanced decision-making
capabilities and personalized customer interactions achiev-
able through sophisticated ML models and AI-powered
systems [14].
Recent studies have explored enhancements to smart
shopping carts through more advanced AI and ML models.
For example, AI-driven shopping systems have been devel-
oped for real-time product recommendations, automated
pathfinding, and predictive analytics for inventory manage-
ment [15]. While these innovations mark progress, they are
often limited to isolated functions such as automated billing
or static navigation, without fully exploiting AI’s potential
for adaptive, real-time decision-making in dynamic retail
environments.
Our proposed AI-powered smart shopping cart system
advances these efforts by integrating RL algorithms for

B. AI AND ML IN THE RETAIL SECTOR
AI and ML applications in retail have shown substantial
potential in optimizing both customer experiences and
operational processes [16], [17]. AI is applied across
various retail functions, such as customer behavior analysis,
demand forecasting, and recommendation generation [18].
ML techniques, especially deep learning and RL, play a
crucial role in enabling these systems to continuously learn
from extensive datasets and improve performance over time.
Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), for instance, are
widely used in recommendation systems to predict customer
preferences based on historical purchasing patterns and real-
time interactions [14].
RL, in particular, has proven effective for path opti-
mization, notably within autonomous robotic systems in
warehouses and retail stores [19], [20]. By learning from
the environment, RL models can continuously optimize
navigation paths, reducing travel time and avoiding obstacles,
thereby improving overall operational efficiency. Despite
these advancements, the application of these techniques to
smart shopping carts remains limited, with research often
focusing on discrete aspects such as inventory management
or customer interaction, rather than developing an integrated
system.
This work addresses this gap by presenting a unified
AI-driven smart cart system that combines real-time data
acquisition, autonomous navigation, and predictive inventory
management. The system leverages AI for decision-making
and automation while employing ML models to learn from
customer interactions, dynamically optimizing the shopping
process. By integrating RL for real-time navigation and
LSTM networks for demand forecasting, our system adapts to
both the physical environment and shifting market conditions,
offering an end-to-end solution for retail automation.
C. AUTONOMOUS SYSTEMS FOR CUSTOMER
INTERACTION
Autonomous systems, including robotic assistants and mobile
service robots, are increasingly prevalent in retail and
hospitality [21], [22]. These systems utilize AI for customer
guidance, real-time assistance, and autonomous naviga-
tion [23]. For example, Simbe Robotics’ Tally [24] leverages
AI-driven computer vision and ML to autonomously audit
store shelves, ensuring product availability. While similar
technologies have been applied to autonomous shopping
assistants, few integrate personalized customer interactions
with effective in-store navigation and inventory management.
Our smart shopping cart system enhances these autonomous
capabilities by incorporating personalized recommendations
with real-time customer tracking and interaction. Using
person-specific tracking algorithms, the system reliably
follows customers throughout the store, while RL dynam-
ically optimizes navigation based on store layout and
customer behavior. By merging AI-driven personalization
with autonomous navigation, our system delivers an intuitive
and seamless shopping experience, elevating customer
satisfaction and operational efficiency.
D. CHALLENGES IN AI-DRIVEN RETAIL SYSTEMS
Despite advancements in AI and ML, challenges remain in
deploying AI-driven retail systems. One primary challenge
is integrating AI models with existing retail infrastructure,
such as POS systems, inventory databases, and customer
relationship management platforms [11]. Seamless com-
munication among these systems is essential to achieve
real-time synchronization of customer data, inventory levels,
and product information.
Data privacy and security are also critical, as AI-driven
systems often collect and process substantial volumes of
customer data [25]. Retailers must secure this data and ensure
compliance with privacy regulations. Additionally, scalability
poses a challenge, as AI systems must manage interactions
with hundreds or thousands of customers concurrently
without compromising performance.
This study addresses these challenges by developing a
smart shopping cart system that integrates with existing retail
infrastructure while ensuring data privacy through encrypted
communications and role-based access control. The system is
also highly scalable, capable of supporting multiple carts and
customers simultaneously without performance degradation.
III. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
The architecture consists of three primary layers—Hardware
Layer, Data Processing Layer, and Application Layer—each
fulfilling specific functions to ensure optimal performance
and a seamless customer experience. An overview of these
layers is illustrated in Figure 1.
A. HARDWARE LAYER
The Hardware Layer is responsible for capturing real-time
data and enabling autonomous operations. It integrates
several essential components, including RGB-D cameras that
provide both visual and depth data to support person tracking
and obstacle detection. Ultrasonic sensors enhance obstacle
avoidance capabilities and help maintain safe distances from
customers, while infrared sensors detect line patterns or
predefined routes to aid in navigation. Product identification
is automated through a barcode scanner or RFID reader,
which simplifies item addition as products are placed in the
cart. Motors and controllers manage the cart’s movement
by adjusting speed and direction in response to sensor data.
The onboard microcontroller (Raspberry Pi) processes these
inputs and executes low-latency commands for real-time
control, enabling smooth and responsive navigation.

C. APPLICATION LAYER
The Application Layer provides interfaces for customers and
administrators, allowing real-time interaction with the cart
and the backend system. The customer interface, accessible
via a mobile app or in-store display, offers features such as
personalized product recommendations based on shopping
data, shopping list integration that guides customers through
the store, and automated billing with real-time item scanning
for a seamless checkout experience. The administrator
interface enables store staff to monitor stock levels through
real-time updates and predictive analytics, observe sales
trends for optimizing store layout and promotions, and track
cart performance, including battery life and sensor status.

A. TRACKING ACCURACY
We assess the tracking accuracy of the proposed system,
which combines the Kalman filter with Re-ID, against
standalone Kalman filter and Re-ID approaches. Table 2
and Figure 2 display the results, showing that the combined
method achieved a precision of 0.94, recall of 0.92, and
F1-score of 0.93, surpassing the Kalman filter (F1-score:
0.83) and Re-ID alone (F1-score: 0.86). The ID Switch Rate
(IDSR) for the proposed method is significantly lower at 0.03,
highlighting its robustness in crowded environments.
We performed a statistical t-test to confirm that the preci-
sion and F1-score improvements over standalone methods are
statistically significant (p < 0.05). The findings indicate that
the proposed system can reliably track individual customers
in practical retail scenarios, improving user experience by
reducing misidentifications and interruptions.

C. TRACKING ROBUSTNESS
To assess the system’s robustness, tracking accuracy is tested
under varying conditions such as high crowd density and

maintained an F1-score of 0.90 with a minor increase in the

improve robustness in adverse conditions.
D. NAVIGATION EFFICIENCY AND REAL-TIME COLLISION
AVOIDANCE TESTING WITH DYNAMIC OBSTACLES
The system’s navigation efficiency is evaluated in environ-
ments with static and dynamic obstacles, specifically testing
real-time collision avoidance. The RL-based navigation
system is compared with two established baseline methods:
A* pathfinding and Dynamic Window Approach (DWA).
As shown in Table 4 and Figure 3, the RL-based system
maintained a collision rate below 0.03 and achieved a success
rate of 96% in high-density and dynamic obstacle settings.
The RL-based system outperformed both A* and DWA,
especially in dynamic obstacle scenarios, where it maintained
lower collision rates and shorter path lengths, showcasing its
adaptive capability to avoid obstacles in real-time.
E. NAVIGATION RELIABILITY AND ROBUSTNESS
The navigation system is further evaluated under condi-
tions with varying obstacle density and dynamic obstacles
(e.g., moving individuals). The RL-based navigation system
showed a 96% success rate in high-density obstacle scenarios,
compared to 85% and 89% for A* and DWA, respectively.
The RL approach dynamically adjusted paths in response to
moving obstacles, demonstrating adaptability that is critical
in retail environments with high foot traffic.
F. RECOMMENDATION ACCURACY
The deep learning-based recommendation system outper-
formed collaborative filtering (CF) and matrix factorization
(MF) in terms of Precision@5, Recall@5, and Mean
Reciprocal Rank (MRR). As shown in Table 5 and Figure 4,
the proposed method reached a Precision@5 of 0.88, higher
than CF (0.76) and MF (0.80), with an MRR of 0.84,
demonstrating its ability to provide relevant and personalized
recommendations.
G. REAL-TIME DEMAND FORECASTING IN DYNAMIC
RETAIL SCENARIOS
To evaluate adaptability, the LSTM-based demand forecast-

The LSTM model adjusted within 5 minutes of the demand

H. DEMAND FORECASTING ACCURACY
The LSTM-based demand forecasting model demonstrated
superior accuracy over ARIMA, achieving lower MSE

I. USER SATISFACTION ANALYSIS
User surveys are conducted to evaluate satisfaction with
system features, such as ease of use, recommendation
relevance, and overall shopping experience. Results indicated
an 85% satisfaction rate, with high ratings in ease of use and
recommendation quality. User feedback also highlighted the
convenience of automated billing and the accuracy of real-
time recommendations.
J. SCALABILITY AND SYSTEM LATENCY
The system is tested with up to 50 concurrent carts in
a simulated retail environment. Average system latency
remained below 200 ms, ensuring real-time responsiveness.
Performance scaled effectively, with minor latency increases
observed as the number of carts approached capacity,
indicating robustness under increased loads.

VII. CONCLUSION
In conclusion, the proposed AI-powered smart shopping
cart system successfully demonstrates the transformative
potential of advanced AI and ML technologies in the retail
sector. By integrating person-specific tracking, RL for path
optimization, and LSTM-based demand forecasting, the
system enhances both the shopping experience and oper-
ational efficiency. Through real-time data collection, edge
computing, and cloud-based decision-making, this system
addresses key challenges in modern retail, such as inefficient
inventory management, suboptimal customer experiences,
and manual checkout processes. The experimental results
show significant improvements in customer satisfaction,
operational efficiency, and inventory management when
compared to baseline systems. This system provides a fully
autonomous and adaptive retail solution that not only reduces
costs but also enhances the overall shopping experience
through personalized recommendations and seamless billing
integration.
While our system has demonstrated strong performance
in controlled environments, we acknowledge that real-world
deployment in retail stores may introduce additional chal-
lenges. Factors such as varying lighting conditions, high
customer density, and complex store layouts could influence
tracking accuracy and navigation efficiency. Future work will
focus on large-scale testing in diverse retail settings to assess
these impacts. Additionally, integrating adaptive AI models
that can dynamically adjust to environmental conditions will
further enhance system robustness and usability in real-world
scenarios.
